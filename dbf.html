Carleton's Design/Build/Fly team

Our entry in AIAA's Textron Aviation/Raytheon Design, Build, Fly competition — a year-long project to design, build, and fly a real radio-controlled aircraft against a mission that's brand new every season.

This year's mission: UAV with a towed sensor

The 2026–27 challenge asks teams to design, build, and test an uncrewed aircraft that can carry, deploy, and recover a towed sensor mid-flight.

Across the season, the aircraft has to deliver itself, transport a sensor packed in a shipping container, and then actually fly with a sensor trailing behind it on a line — lighting it up in different modes on command before reeling it back in and landing safely. It's a good example of what DBF asks every year: take a fairly unusual mission profile and turn it into a flyable, judged aircraft in a few months.

WINGSPAN LIMIT
6 ft max
Electric propulsion only, radio-controlled, no autopilot or GPS allowed.
WEIGHT LIMIT
Under 55 lb
Take-off gross weight, including payload — this is an AMA-sanctioned event.
TEAM MIX
All years welcome
At least 1/3 underclassmen; grad students limited to at most 1/3 of the roster.

The missions

Teams fly three timed missions plus a ground mission, each building on the last.

  1. Staging Flight

    A clean baseline flight — three laps, no payload, within a 5-minute window.

  2. Delivery Flight

    Same course, but now carrying the sensor in its shipping container (plus optional weighted simulators) — scored on how much weight it carries per second of flight time.

  3. Sensor Flight

    The aircraft deploys the towed sensor mid-flight, cycles its lights through three modes on command each lap, then stows it again before landing — scored on laps flown and sensor weight.

  4. Ground Mission

    A separate test of the shipping container itself: it has to survive a series of drop tests onto concrete without damaging the sensor inside.
