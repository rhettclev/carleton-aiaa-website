Where the ideas fly

AIAA runs hands-on design competitions that take students from a blank page to a working aircraft, satellite, or proposal. Our flagship effort is Design/Build/Fly.

FLAGSHIP COMPETITION

Design/Build/Fly (DBF)

Now in its fourth decade, DBF challenges student teams to design, fabricate, and fly an uncrewed, electric-powered, radio-controlled aircraft against a mission profile that changes every year. Teams are judged on a written design report and flight performance at the fly-off.

It's as close as most undergrads get to a full aircraft development cycle: requirements, trade studies, structural design, manufacturing, integration, and flight test, all against a hard deadline and a live audience of judges.

Beyond DBF

DBF isn't the only way to compete. AIAA runs a wider slate of competitions students can get involved with.

AIAA Design Competitions

Paper design competitions modeled on real industry Requests for Proposal (RFPs) — a good fit if you'd rather focus on the engineering write-up than the physical build. They run across several categories:

Aircraft Design (Team & Individual) · Missile Systems Design · Space Design · Engine Design

CanSat

A design-build-launch challenge where teams build a satellite-like payload sized to fit inside a soft-drink can, then launch, recover, and analyze it against a mission profile — run jointly with the American Astronautical Society.

Other teams in the branch

DBF isn't the only build project connected to Carleton AIAA — some teams run their own independent programs and sites.

Blackbird UAV

Carleton's entry in the AEAC Student Competition (Aerial Evolution Association of Canada) — a national challenge to design, build, and operate uncrewed aerial systems for real-world missions. Blackbird runs as its own team within the branch, with its own site and vehicle lineup.
